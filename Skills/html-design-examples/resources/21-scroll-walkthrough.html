A diagram the reader scrolls through

A pinned diagram turns a long explanation into one picture. Scrolling moves a camera over it, lights one part at a time and runs a short animation that the reader can stop, reverse or skip. This page documents that style by using it. The walkthrough below draws the scroll engine that is driving it, with live values.

Look

Everything is monospaced. Labels, axes and buttons are small tracked capitals in Kode Mono, sentences are IBM Plex Mono. Figures sit bare on the page under a rule. Depth comes from a flat offset shadow. Click a colour to copy it.

How a frame is drawn

These stages run on each animation frame while the page is moving. Only the panel waits for a step change. Scroll through them, use Prev and Next, or select any part.

Why scrubbing beats triggering

Many scroll animations start a timer when an element enters the viewport. That works for a chart drawing itself once. It fails for an explanation, because the reader moves both ways.

Anatomy of one step

A step owns a stretch of scroll. The camera arrives in the first 12%, the animation plays over the next 72%, and the camera leaves over the last 16%. Underlined phrases and the strip highlight each other.

Motion grammar

The lag filter smooths wheel ticks into continuous movement. Interface transitions use cubic-bezier(.22, 1, .36, 1). Scroll-scrubbed movement uses smoothstep, because it is symmetric and reads the same in reverse.
